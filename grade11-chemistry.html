Types of Chemical Reactions

In a chemical reaction, starting substances (reactants) turn into new substances (products) with different properties. Evidence includes gas bubbles, a color change, heat or light, a new smell, or a solid forming.

✂️ DecompositionOne substance breaks into simpler ones.
AB → A + B
2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂
🧪 Acid + carbonateMakes a salt, water, and carbon dioxide gas.
Chalk or eggshell in vinegar fizzes.
🔩 Acid + metalMakes a salt and hydrogen gas.
Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂
🔥 CombustionA substance reacts rapidly with oxygen, releasing heat and light.
Fuel + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O
Identifying a gas: the quick tests
  • Hydrogen: a lit splint at the mouth of the tube gives a squeaky “pop.”
  • Carbon dioxide: turns limewater milky or cloudy.
  • Oxygen: relights a glowing splint.
💡 Match observation to evidence. A “pop” means hydrogen, not oxygen or CO₂. “Bubbling” alone doesn’t tell you which gas.
Exothermic or endothermic?

Exothermic reactions release energy (heat, light). They feel warm or hot. Endothermic reactions absorb energy and may feel cold. Combustion releases heat and light, so it is exothermic.

Spot the type in an equation
  • One reactant, several products → decomposition.
  • Fuel + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O → combustion.
  • Metal + acid → salt + H₂ → acid on metal.
  • Carbonate + acid → salt + H₂O + CO₂ → acid on carbonate.

Chemical Reactions in Daily Life

🍞 BakingBaking soda releases CO₂ when heated (decomposition), and baking powder releases it when wet and heated. The trapped gas makes batter rise.
🧽 CleaningVinegar or lemon juice (acids) react with limescale (calcium carbonate), producing a salt, water, and CO₂.
🔥 BurningPropane in an LPG stove, charcoal, wood, and candle wax burn in oxygen, forming CO₂ and water (with heat and light).
🔩 RustingIron reacts slowly with oxygen and moisture to form reddish-brown flaky iron oxide.
Physical change or chemical change?

Ask: is a new substance formed? Sugar dissolving in water is physical because you can get the sugar back. Limescale “disappearing” in vinegar is chemical, because the scale reacted and became new substances, including a gas.

💡 “It disappeared” or “it looks shiny now” doesn’t prove a physical change. Look for evidence of new substances.
Fast vs. slow reactions

Combustion is fast (heat and light), while rusting is slow (days or weeks). Both are oxidation reactions involving oxygen.

Metabolism

Metabolism is all the chemical reactions in the cells of a living thing that build up or break down substances. Your cells do this all the time, even when you rest or sleep.

⬇️ CatabolicBreak big molecules into smaller ones and release energy.
Glucose + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + energy (cellular respiration).
⬆️ AnabolicBuild big molecules from small ones and use energy.
Amino acids → protein.
Enzymes: the speed-up helpers

Enzymes are proteins that speed up metabolic reactions without being used up, so each enzyme can work again and again. Without them, reactions in cells would be too slow to sustain life.

Why exercise changes your breathing
🏃 muscles work harder➜⚡ more energy needed➜🫁 respiration speeds up➜💨 oxygen need increases

Faster respiration also makes more CO₂, so you breathe faster and your heart beats faster.

Common mix-ups
  • “Resting means no metabolism”: No. Cells keep reacting to stay alive.
  • “Metabolism = digestion only”: No. It’s every reaction in cells, building and breaking.
  • “Catabolic stores energy”: No. Catabolic releases it.

Solutions

A solution is a homogeneous mixture: it looks the same all the way through. The solute is what dissolves, and the solvent is the part present in the greater amount that does the dissolving. In salt water, salt is the solute and water is the solvent.

🫧 SolutionTiny particles. Clear or uniform. Does not settle. Passes through filter paper.
🥛 ColloidMedium particles. Looks uniform, but a light beam shows a path (milk, fog). Doesn’t settle.
🌪️ SuspensionLarge particles. Cloudy. Settles on standing. Filter can trap the particles (muddy water).
Solutions are everywhere
  • Household: vinegar, saline solution, soft drinks, sweetened tea.
  • Industry: alloys are solid solutions of metals, such as brass (copper + zinc), steel (iron + carbon), and bronze.
  • Environment: seawater (salts in water) and air (gases mixed in gas).

Solutions can be solid, liquid, or gas. Brass is a solid solution, not a liquid.

“Both are mixtures” doesn’t mean “both are solutions”

Every solution is a mixture, but not every mixture is a solution. A solution must be uniform throughout. Oil and water separate into layers, and muddy water settles, so neither is a solution.

Handling, Storage, and Disposal of Chemicals

🥽 HandlingWear goggles and gloves. Work in a ventilated area. Read the label and SDS first. Never taste or sniff chemicals. Add acid slowly to water, never water into concentrated acid.
🗄️ StorageLabel every container with its name and hazard. Store incompatible chemicals apart (for example bleach away from ammonia cleaners or acids). Keep cabinets closed and cool.
🗑️ DisposalUse labeled waste containers. Don’t pour chemicals down the drain or throw them in ordinary trash. Follow school and local rules.
What’s an SDS?

A Safety Data Sheet (SDS) gives a chemical’s hazards, safe handling, storage, and first-aid measures. Check it before using an unfamiliar chemical.

Why we separate chemicals

Some chemicals react dangerously. Bleach mixed with ammonia or an acid can release toxic gas, even from a small leak. Storing incompatible items apart prevents fires, burns, toxic gas, and pollution of water and soil.

💡 “Small bottles are always fine together” and “both are household cleaners” are traps. Check what could happen if they mix.

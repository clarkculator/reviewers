Biological Systems & Structure-Function

A biological system is a group of related parts that work together to carry out a function. Life is organized in levels:

🔬 cell➜🧱 tissue➜🫀 organ➜🧍 organ system➜🌿 organism

A tissue is a group of similar cells working together on one job.

Structure fits function

The shape of a part usually tells you its job. Exchange and absorption structures tend to be thin (short trip for materials), folded or extended (more surface area), and supplied with tiny blood vessels (in animals).

🫁 AlveoliThin walls + many capillaries: swap oxygen and CO₂
🍝 VilliFinger-like folds + thin walls: absorb nutrients
🌱 Root hairsLong thin extensions: absorb water and minerals
💡 Different parts, same big idea: thin and spread out means good at exchange.
Systems team up

Body systems don’t work alone. When a hiker climbs a steep trail, the respiratory system takes in more oxygen while the circulatory system pumps it faster to the muscles. In an asthma attack, the airways narrow, less oxygen gets in, and the heart beats faster to compensate.

Cell Energy & Respiration

Cellular respiration happens in the mitochondria (the cell’s “powerhouses”). Glucose and oxygen are used to make ATP, the energy-carrying molecule cells use to power their activities.

glucose + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + ATP

Plants respire too

Plants make glucose by photosynthesis in chloroplasts, but they still need to break glucose down in mitochondria to release usable energy. Photosynthesis stores energy; respiration releases it.

💡 “Plants don’t need respiration” is a classic trap. Every living cell needs ATP.
No oxygen? Lactic acid fermentation

During a hard sprint, muscle cells can run short of oxygen. They switch to lactic acid fermentation to keep releasing some energy (much less than aerobic respiration). It makes lactic acid.

Which cells have the most mitochondria?

Cells that need lots of energy, like muscle cells and heart cells, contain more mitochondria than less active cells.

How Plants Move Water

💧 XylemCarries water and minerals up from roots to leaves. Tube-like, hollow cells form a continuous pipeline.
🍬 PhloemCarries sugar made in leaves to other parts of the plant.
🛡️ EpidermisOuter protective layer of the plant.
🌱 CambiumMakes new xylem and phloem so the stem can grow thicker.
Transpiration: the leaf pulls
🌱 roots absorb water➜🪵 stem’s xylem carries it up➜🍃 leaves lose water vapor (transpiration)➜⬆️ pulls more water up

Transpiration is the loss of water vapor through tiny openings in leaves. As vapor escapes, it pulls water upward through the xylem. The tube-like, continuous shape of xylem lets water flow in an unbroken column.

The colored-water experiment

When a stalk stands in dyed water, the tiny veins turn colored because the dye travels up the xylem with the water. The leaves didn’t make the dye, and phloem carries sugar, not water from the soil.

Defense & Vaccines

The immune system recognizes and fights germs. After an infection or vaccination, it makes antibodies and memory cells. If the same germ shows up again, memory cells recognize it and respond faster and stronger.

How vaccines work
  • Vaccines use weakened, inactivated, or partial germs that cannot cause the full disease.
  • They prepare the immune system to recognize and fight the real germ in the future.
  • The protection comes from memory cells, not from the vaccine staying in the blood or killing germs directly.
Fixing misconceptions
  • “The vaccine gives you the disease”: No. The germs are weakened, inactivated, or only a piece.
  • “Vaccines kill germs in the blood”: No. They train the immune system.
  • “Antibodies last for life without memory cells”: No. Memory cells make future responses fast.

Staying Balanced: Heat, Hormones, Joints

Cooling down: the response chain
🌡️ skin receptors sense heat➜🧠 hypothalamus processes➜💦 sweat glands release sweat

Sweat cools you because it takes heat away from the skin as it evaporates. Blood vessels in the skin also widen so more heat can be lost.

💡 Sensor → processor → responder. The sequence order matters.
Preventing heat stress

Take breaks in the shade, wear light, loose clothes, and drink water often to replace lost sweat. Avoid working in the hottest hours without rest, and avoid tight, thick, dark clothes.

Hormones

Hormones are chemical messengers made by endocrine glands and carried by the blood to their target organs. Insulin from the pancreas is one example.

Bones, ligaments, and tendons
  • Ligament: tough connective tissue joining bone to bone, keeping a joint stable (like the ACL in the knee).
  • Tendon: joins muscle to bone.

If a ligament tears, the joint becomes unsteady, and the muscles alone can’t fully stabilize it.
